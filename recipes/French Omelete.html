French Omelete

French omelette originated from ancient egg dishes in Persia and Europe, evolving into its refined, pale, and creamy style in France during the 17th and 18th centuries. The ingredients for this dish are
  • Eggs: 2 to 3 large, fresh eggs per serving.
  • Butter: 1 tablespoon of unsalted butter (plus an extra touch to coat the pan or glaze the outside).
  • Salt: A pinch of fine salt to season and tenderize the eggs.
  • Black or White Pepper: A light dash, added to taste.

How to prepare

  1. Beat the eggs: Crack the eggs into a bowl. Use a fork to beat them well until the whites and yolks are fully combined without adding too much air. Season with a pinch of salt.
  2. Heat the pan: Place your non-stick pan over medium heat. Add the butter and let it melt until it foams up, making sure it does not turn brown.
  3. Scramble quickly: Pour the eggs into the pan all at once. Stir and shake the pan constantly with your fork or spatula to break the eggs into tiny curds, mimicking a soft scramble.

  4. Set the base: When a soft, creamy layer forms and the edges begin to set, smooth the eggs into an even sheet. Turn off or lower the heat.

  5. Roll the omelette: Fold the top third of the omelette down toward the middle. Roll it over again into a neat cylinder shape.
